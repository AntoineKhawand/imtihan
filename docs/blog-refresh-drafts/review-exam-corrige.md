An exam generator with corrigé is most useful when the questions and worked answers are reviewed together. Generating a solution at the same time as a question saves a separate drafting step, but it does not establish that the answer is correct or that every mark is justified. Start with an explicit teacher brief, solve the paper independently, and check the student export before distribution.

This guide focuses on the review stage for mathematics, physics and chemistry teachers. It applies whether your course follows Bac Libanais, Bac Français, IB or a university syllabus: the generated paper is a practice draft, and the teacher owns the final assessment decisions.

## What a useful corrigé should contain

A good worked answer explains the method, not just the final value. It should state assumptions, show the necessary calculations, use coherent units and connect the conclusion to the question. A marking guide should then identify what earns credit at each stage.

For an explanation question, decide what reasoning is necessary for a complete answer. For a calculation, check whether method, substitution, units and interpretation are separate criteria or part of one combined criterion. Avoid awarding the same piece of evidence twice.

Official-board conventions need a separate check. For example, an [IB-style mark scheme](/blog/ib-mark-scheme-generator) should be compared with the current subject guide and specimen marking guidance. Generated criteria are not official approval, and error-carried-forward treatment should not be applied as a blanket rule.

## A small example: a correct calculation can still be incomplete

Consider this illustrative mathematics question: solve x² − 5x + 6 = 0 for 1 < x < 2.5, explaining your conclusion.

The factorization is (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0, so the unrestricted solutions are 2 and 3. The interval restriction leaves only x = 2. An answer key that lists both roots has performed the algebra but failed to answer the full question.

An illustrative four-mark classroom rubric could award two marks for a valid algebraic method, one for obtaining the roots and one for applying the interval restriction. Adapt that allocation to your teaching objectives and the expected method; it is not an official board rubric.

This example also shows why reading only the final numeric answer is insufficient. The corrigé must preserve every condition in the statement, including domain restrictions and assumptions that appear earlier in a multi-part problem.

## Check the data before checking the answer

For science questions, inspect the supplied measurements and units. Check that tables contain enough information to attempt the task and that the values are physically or chemically plausible. A beautifully formatted table cannot rescue an inconsistent experimental setup.

Ask whether the data accidentally reveals the desired conclusion. If a task is supposed to assess model selection, an annotation naming the correct model defeats the purpose. Use clear labels for quantities and units, but let students justify the interpretation.

If a graph is required, review the actual plotted output and axis labels. Then check that the corrigé discusses the graph students can see, rather than a different relationship described only in the generated solution.

## Make your brief request a reviewable answer key

Try this teacher prompt, adapted to your selected chapters and level:

> Create a classroom assessment with a worked corrigé. State the assumptions, show the reasoning needed for each answer and allocate marks to distinct criteria. Include acceptable alternative methods where appropriate. Keep answer-revealing information out of the student statement, and flag any marking convention I need to verify against my subject guide.

After generation, replace or revise any question whose solution depends on an assumption that students were never given. Recheck the total marks after editing so the question paper and corrigé remain aligned.

## Export two files and inspect both

Use the corrigé option to produce a student paper without answers and a teacher copy with worked solutions. Open the exported files rather than relying only on the preview. Check headings, numbering, diagrams, tables, mark totals and page breaks.

Finally, make sure the student file contains no answers in captions, question titles or copied source annotations. Once both versions are reviewed, the corrigé becomes a useful feedback tool rather than an unchecked authority.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Does a generated corrigé guarantee correct answers?**
No. Solve the questions independently and check the worked reasoning, conditions, units and mark allocations before classroom use.

**Q: Can I export a student paper without the answers?**
Yes. Turn off the corrigé option for the student export and use a separate answer-inclusive teacher copy. Inspect both exported files before distribution.

**Q: Why should I check an answer that has the right final number?**
The method may rely on an unstated assumption, omit units, ignore a domain restriction or fail to justify the conclusion. Those issues matter even when a numeric value happens to match.
