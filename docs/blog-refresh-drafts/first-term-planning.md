First-term exam planning starts with what your class has actually learned. Decide which objectives to assess, how much time students have, and what evidence earns each mark before drafting questions. An AI-generated paper can help with the first draft; the teacher still needs to solve it and check the marking guide.

In a Lebanese school offering Bac Libanais, Bac Français or IB classes, the curriculum name alone is not a sufficient brief. Two classes may have covered different chapters or used different notation. This checklist helps you turn your teaching sequence into an assessment that students can understand and teachers can mark consistently.

## 1. Build a brief from taught objectives

List the chapters already taught, then write a short objective for each. “Functions” is a topic; “interpret a graph and justify the number of solutions on a stated interval” is an assessable objective. Mark any prerequisite that students need before they can attempt the problem.

For a classroom paper, agree on the duration, available resources and total marks. If you want a particular official-paper composition, consult the current board documents and review the structure explicitly. Do not assume that an AI tool has reproduced the correct official format simply because you selected a curriculum.

Confirm the level and selected chapters in the creation process before generating.

## 2. Decide what “hard” means in your classroom

A difficult question should demand reasoning within the taught syllabus. Long arithmetic or an unfamiliar chapter can make a paper difficult for the wrong reason. Specify whether students should connect two ideas, select a method without a hint, interpret evidence or justify an assumption.

For example, a physics teacher might ask students to compare two explanations of a set of measurements. The challenge comes from choosing and defending a model. It should not come from missing units, unexplained notation or experimental values that cannot occur together.

Imtihan starts with challenging questions, but you should review the difficulty settings and read every exercise. Adjust the draft for your class rather than treating the default as a judgment about what your students should already be able to do.

## 3. Use uploaded documents deliberately

An uploaded course document can supply content. A past paper can inspire exercise ideas or guide the composition. A school document can provide header and footer details. These are different uses: tell the generator which ones you intend.

If you want fresh exercises, select exercise inspiration and check that the generated questions assess the same skill without copying the original. If you want a particular section arrangement, select structure and review the extracted sections, marks and instructions before generating. Remove any irrelevant source document rather than leaving the AI to guess why it is attached.

## 4. Review the student paper and corrigé together

Solve each question without consulting the generated answer first. Then compare your solution with the corrigé. Look for missing assumptions, alternative valid methods, unit conversions and mark criteria that award credit twice for the same reasoning.

Time the paper with your class in mind. Your own solving time is useful context, but it is not a direct measure of student completion time. Include reading, interpreting graphs and writing explanations when judging whether the paper is feasible.

## 5. Check the export before distribution

Review the chapter coverage, total marks, numbering and optional-choice instructions. Export a student version without the corrigé and a separate teacher version with answers. Open the actual Word or PDF file and inspect tables, diagrams, page breaks and the school information.

A final checklist:

- Every question stays inside the taught objectives.
- All necessary measurements, units and assumptions are present.
- The marks match the expected reasoning.
- The worked answers have been checked independently.
- The student file contains no solutions or answer-revealing annotations.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Does selecting Bac Libanais or IB guarantee the official exam structure?**
No. Review the selected composition and current board requirements. Imtihan's built-in reference compositions cover selected mathematics profiles; other subjects need a teacher-defined or uploaded structure.

**Q: Can I use the same paper for two parallel classes?**
You can start from one shared brief, but compare taught content, difficulty and marking criteria before using the papers. Matching total marks alone does not establish equal difficulty.

**Q: Should students receive the generated corrigé?**
Keep it separate during the assessment. Once the work is complete, a teacher-checked solution can support feedback and discussion of reasoning, not only the final score.
